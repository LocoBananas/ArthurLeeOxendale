Current Statement

Accountability, documentation and next steps

In my experience, Arthur Lee Oxendale maintained multiple overlapping “relationships” while presenting ours as exclusive. I believe there was a repeated pattern in which he received money, transportation, housing, or other resources through these relationships, and I have documentation that appears to support that belief.

What it looks like to me is that he moves from woman to woman and relies on those relationships for financial and material support—including help with living expenses, transportation, money, and partying. That is how the pattern appears to me based on what I personally experienced and the information I have documented.

During our nearly three-year relationship, I experienced what I believe were multiple forms of abuse, along with substantial financial and property loss. I allowed him to use my car after he explicitly promised to return it in better condition. Instead, it was returned barely drivable and now needs tires, rear brakes, shocks, radiator repairs, and diagnosis of an oil leak—none of which, to my knowledge, were present when I entrusted the car to him. There is exterior and interior damage that I believe occurred while it was in his possession as well.

I am preserving the available evidence and intend to pursue repayment and accountability through appropriate legal channels. This statement reflects my personal experience, beliefs, and interpretation of the documented pattern; it is not a court finding. It may be updated as additional evidence is organized or circumstances change.

I just couldn't believe it.

This is the story of my relationship with Arthur Lee Oxendale, told partly through the words I wrote while I was still living it. It wasn't a clean journey from believing to knowing to leaving. For a long time, I knew things that I still couldn't make myself fully believe. Even now, my heart searches for reasonable explanations—anything that could explain all of this in a kinder, gentler way, without the glaring neon sign that says, “I got played.” If he were to appear before me right now and apologize, I can't say for sure what I would do. This is the result of a trauma bond. And it is said that no other addiction is more difficult to break.

Why didn't I just leave?

People ask that question about victims all the time. Looking back, I think the better question is: what was happening that made accepting the truth — and trusting my own perception of it — so difficult?

I didn't simply believe everything until one day I discovered the truth. I noticed things. I questioned things. Sometimes I knew. Then I doubted what I knew, defended him, hoped I was wrong, forgave, tried again, and found myself confronting the same truths later.

The writings below matter because many weren't written with hindsight. They show what I actually thought and felt while I was inside the relationship.
