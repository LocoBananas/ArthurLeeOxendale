Current Statement

Accountability, documentation and next steps

In my experience, Arthur Lee Oxendale maintained multiple overlapping “relationships” while presenting ours as exclusive. I have documentation indicating that he received money, transportation, housing, or other resources through these relationships.

During our nearly three-year relationship, I experienced multiple forms of abuse and substantial financial and property loss. I allowed him to use my car after he explicitly promised to return it in better condition. Instead, it was returned barely drivable and now needs tires, rear brakes, shocks, radiator repairs, and diagnosis of an oil leak—none of which were present when I entrusted the car to him. There is exterior and interior damage that was done as well.

I am preserving the available evidence and intend to pursue repayment and accountability through appropriate legal channels. This statement reflects my personal experience and allegations supported by documentation; it is not a court finding. It may be updated as additional evidence is organized or circumstances change.

Somewhere inside, I knew.
I just couldn't believe it.

This is the story of my relationship with Arthur Lee Oxendale, told partly through the words I wrote while I was still living it. It wasn't a clean journey from believing to knowing to leaving. For a long time, I knew things that I still couldn't make myself fully believe. Even now, my heart searches for reasonable explanations—anything that could explain all of this in a kinder, gentler way, without the glaring neon sign that says, “I got played.” If he were to appear before me right now and apologize, I can't say for sure what I would do. That is the power of a trauma bond, and trauma bonds can be incredibly difficult to break.

Why didn't I just leave?

People ask that question about victims all the time. Looking back, I think the better question is: what was happening that made accepting the truth — and trusting my own perception of it — so difficult?

I didn't simply believe everything until one day I discovered the truth. I noticed things. I questioned things. Sometimes I knew. Then I doubted what I knew, defended him, hoped I was wrong, forgave, tried again, and found myself confronting the same truths later.

The writings below matter because many weren't written with hindsight. They show what I actually thought and felt while I was inside the relationship.

The Second Facebook Account

The account that was supposed to be “ours.”

We created another Facebook account intending it to be something we shared as a couple. That never became what I expected it to be. Eventually I took the account and began using it to write about what was happening in the relationship.

There is an irony in that I can't miss now: a space I thought would represent us became one of the places where I documented what “us” actually felt like from inside it.

Why This Exists

I'm not putting myself on trial.

I still fear being judged for how long I stayed, how often I hoped, and how long I could know something without being ready to fully believe it. But I don't want to edit those contradictions out of my story to make myself easier for strangers to understand.

So I'm leaving them in.

This archive will grow as I recover surviving Facebook posts, journals, screenshots and other writings. Missing pieces will be labeled as missing. Memories will be called memories. Documentation will be called documentation. I don't need to make the story cleaner than it was.
